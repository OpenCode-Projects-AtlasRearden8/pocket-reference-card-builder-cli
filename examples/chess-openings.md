# Chess Openings Pocket Deck

Opening study chunks for club players - ideas first, just enough theory to reach move 10. Verify against a database when prepping for tournaments.

---

# Italian Game (1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.Bc4)

- Idea: fast development, Bc4 eyes the weak f7 square
- Quiet plan: c3 and d4 to build a full center slowly
- Sharp plan: Ng5 hits f7 - watch for ...d5 and ...Ne5 counters
- For Black: ...Bc5 mirrors the plan or plays ...Nf6 into a quiet game
- Endgame goal: keep the bishop pair, trade queens early when winning

---

# Ruy Lopez (1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.Bb5)

- Idea: pressure the knight that guards e5
- 3...a6 is the Morphy Defense - bishop retreats a4 or takes on c6
- Closed systems: slow maneuvering, pawn breaks with d2-d4
- Exchange Variation: doubled c-pawns shape a long structural plan
- Wins decided in rook endings - practice those R+P fundamentals

---

# Sicilian Defense (1.e4 c5)

- Idea: black trades a wing pawn for central counterplay
- Najdorf (...d6, ...a6): flexible - meets anything, waits on e5 or e6
- Dragon (...g6): opposite castling means racing - count tempi first
- Alapin (White 2.c3): blunt the Sicilian with a big d4 center
- Outposts: ...a5-a4 and ...b5-b4 for queenside space

---

# Caro-Kann (1.e4 c6)

- Idea: solid ...d5 push that keeps the light bishop from prison
- Classical: ...Bf5 comes out first, then trade it for a safe structure
- Advance variation: White gains space, Black counters with c6-c5
- Exchange line: near-equal and drawish - fine for must-not-lose games
- Budget: learn 2 lines deep, rely on structure over memory

---

# French Defense (1.e4 e6 2.d4 d5)

- Idea: challenge d4 from the wing, accept a slightly cramped center
- Winawer (3.Nc3 Bb4): pin pressure against the c3 knight, bishop pair sacrifice motifs
- Tarrasch (3.Nd2): White avoids the pin before it starts
- Advance French: e5 vs f6 space race, chains decide tempi
- Watch: the light bishop is buried with ...e6 - unbury it or suffer

---

# Queen's Gambit (1.d4 d5 2.c4)

- Idea: not a true gambit - White usually regains the c4 pawn
- Declined: ...e6/...Nf6/...Be7/0-0 classic solid system
- Accepted: ...dxc4, then strike the center with ...c5 or ...e5
- Slav (...c6): keeps the light bishop free before ...e6
- Key pawn tension: c4xd5 vs ...e6xd5 changes the whole middlegame

---

# London System (1.d4 d5 2.Bf4)

- Idea: one low-theory setup vs almost everything
- Standard build: e3, Nf3, Bd3, Nbd2, c3 - castle short
- Attack plan: Ne5 hitting c6/f7-style weak squares, h4-h5 support
- Vs a kingside fianchetto: central d4-c3 pawn duo stands strong
- Downsides: predictable - know one anti-London answer for Black

---

# King's Indian Defense (1.d4 Nf6 2.c4 g6)

- Idea: concede the big center, then blow it up with ...e5 or ...c5
- Classical: ...Be7/0-0, break with ...e5 when d4 is over-extended
- Fianchetto lines: fight for the dark squares around d6/e5
- f5-f4-g5 pawn storm comes when White castles kingside
- Learn plans, not moves - this opening is a shape, not a script

---

# Practice Routine

- Front of card = move order; back = the one middlegame plan
- Recite the moves aloud, then play 10 blitz games in that line
- Log one lesson per session back into the deck before bed
