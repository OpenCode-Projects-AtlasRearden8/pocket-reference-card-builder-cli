# Nurse Lab Values

Educational study aid for nursing students and RNs - review the deck chunks below. Not medical advice: verify every value against your facility's own reference ranges before any clinical use.

---

# Complete Blood Count (CBC)

- WBC: 5,000-10,000/mmcu (3,900-11,500 lab-dependent) - fights infection
- RBC: 4.5-5.9 men / 4.1-5.1 women million/mcl - oxygen carriers
- Hemoglobin: 14-18 men / 12-16 women g/dL - carries oxygen, flags bleeding
- Hematocrit: 42-52% men / 36-48% women - % of blood that is RBCs
- Platelets: 150,000-400,000/mcl - clotting; low = bleeding risk
- Reticulocytes: 0.5-2.5% - bone marrow output check

---

# Basic Metabolic Panel (BMP)

- Sodium: 135-145 mEq/L - hydration, confusion when abnormal
- Potassium: 3.5-5.0 mEq/L - cardiac rhythm; treat as highest priority value
- Chloride: 95-105 mEq/L - acid/base partner of sodium
- Bicarbonate (HCO3): 22-26 mEq/L - metabolic acid/base
- BUN: 7-20 mg/dL - kidneys, dehydration, protein load
- Creatinine: 0.6-1.2 mg/dL - kidney filter (smaller range women)
- Glucose: 70-100 mg/dL fasting - hypoglycemia under 70, diabetic care goals

---

# Coagulation (PT / PTT / INR)

- PT: 11-12.5 seconds - extrinsic cascade, warfarin watch
- INR: 0.8-1.2 normal / 2.0-3.0 on warfarin (2.5-3.5 mechanical valve)
- PTT: 30-40 seconds - heparin watch (1.5-2.0x control on drip)
- D-dimer: under 0.5 mcg/mL - clot break-down screen (rules out, never rules in)
- Fibrinogen: 200-400 mg/dL - converts to fibrin; DIC check

---

# Cardiac and Hepatic Markers

- Troponin I: under 0.04 ng/mL - heart muscle injury; rises in 3-4 hrs, peaks ~24 hrs
- CK-MB: 0-3 ng/mL - heart muscle leak
- AST: 8-20 units/L / ALT: 7-56 units/L - liver enzymes
- ALP: 45-115 units/L - bone/liver
- Ammonia: 15-45 mcg/dL - hepatic encephalopathy flag
- Bilirubin total: 0.3-1.2 mg/dL - jaundice, hemolysis

---

# Pearls the CI Teaches

- K+ (potassium) abnormal = hook the resident up, call the provider first
- Troponin is a trend: serial draws q6-8h beat one number
- Low H/H after surgery: think bleeding before you think anemia of any chronic sort
- Zero-difference rule is a myth: tiny K+ shifts can flatten a T wave
- Always verify ranges against the lab's own printed reference sheet

---

# Study Routine

- One chunk per day off a fresh card during shift gaps
- Recite the value aloud before you look at the answer side
- Keep the deck in scrub pocket, backside notes = red flags
